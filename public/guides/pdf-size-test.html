We compressed one scanned PDF to 50KB, 100KB, 200KB, 300KB, 500KB, 1MB and 2MB: what each size does

By SlimIO · Updated October 30, 2026 · 4 min read

Every size limit sounds like a number, but what you get at that number is a picture. To see it, we took one made-up document, a 3-page phone-scan style PDF of 3.61 MB, and ran it through each of SlimIO's size pages on 30 October 2026, the same way you would: drop the file in, click Compress, download. This page shows what came out at 50KB, 100KB, 200KB, 300KB, 500KB, 1MB and 2MB.

What the numbers say

Below about 30 KB a page, the body text goes

At 50KB the three pages share 46.5 KB, so each gets about 15 KB. To fit, SlimIO turned every page into an image at about 40 dpi. The large heading survived, but the paragraph text smudged into grey strokes, and because the pages are now pictures, the text can no longer be selected or searched. This is why the 50KB page tells you to keep only the page the form needs: one page gets the whole 50KB.

100KB to 300KB is where scans become usable

With 32 KB a page the text at 100KB was readable but soft. At 200KB (62 KB a page) it was clear, and at 300KB it stayed clean. If a form accepts a few pages and gives you 200KB or more, you can send all three. If it caps you at 100KB, send one or two.

Past 500KB the tool stops rebuilding the pages

At 500KB and 2MB the result was smaller than the limit, because SlimIO could keep the pages exactly as they were and recompress only the pictures inside them. Nothing was rendered at a lower resolution, and the text looked sharper than at 300KB even though the file was only 60 KB bigger. The 2MB result, 1.75 MB, is about half the original with no visible loss.

The 1MB result took the other route

The same file at 1MB did not take that gentle route. The pages were turned into images at about 108 dpi, giving 751.6 KB, and the text, while readable, is a little rougher than the 500KB result. We report it as it came out. Which route a file takes depends on the file, and the result box under the button always says whether the pages were kept or turned into images.

Picking a size for your own scan

Use the per-page column as a rough rule, not a promise:

  • About 15 KB a page: only a heading or a large number survives. Fine for a one-page certificate where you only need it to be recognisable.
  • About 30 KB a page: readable body text, a little soft. A good limit for one or two pages.
  • 60 KB a page or more: clear text. Aim for this when a form checks details like numbers and dates.

If a document is too long for the limit, remove the pages the form does not need or split the PDF before compressing, because every page you drop gives the others more room. Scanning in grayscale at 150 dpi instead of colour also helps; see how to compress a PDF without losing quality for why.

How we ran the test, and its limits

  • The file: three page images (about 1654 × 2339 pixels each, with a printed heading, text lines, a table, a stamp, uneven lighting and sensor-style noise) built into one PDF of 3,781,587 bytes (3.61 MB). The document is made up, so no real person's papers were involved.
  • The run: each size page opened in Chrome against the live tool, the file added, Compress clicked once, the download measured. Sizes are shown the way the tool shows them, in KB of 1,024 bytes.
  • How readable: judged by eye on the first page, at the same zoom, as shown in the picture.
  • What it is not: one file, one day. A different scan, or a later version of the tool, can give different numbers. Treat the pattern as the finding, not the exact kilobytes.

Questions

How many scanned pages fit in 100KB?

In our test, 100KB gave about 32 KB per page for three pages, at roughly 50 dpi. The body text was readable but soft. One or two pages are comfortable at 100KB; three start to lose small print.

Why was the 500KB result sharper than the 300KB one?

At 500KB the tool could keep the three pages as they were and only recompress the pictures inside (340.1 KB), so no page was rebuilt at a lower resolution. At 300KB it had to turn the pages into images at about 90 dpi. Which route a file takes depends on the file.

Does a text PDF behave the same way?

Not exactly. A PDF made in Word keeps its text as characters, which stay sharp and selectable and are tiny to begin with. This test used a scan, where every page is a picture, which is the hard case.

Will my own file give the same numbers?

No. The numbers belong to one made-up document. A scan with photos, shadows or colour needs more room. The point of the test is the pattern: about 15 KB a page is the floor for blurry, about 30 KB for soft, and above 60 KB a page the text is clear.
