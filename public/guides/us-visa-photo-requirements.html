US visa photo requirements for the DS-160: 600×600, 240KB and why uploads fail

By SlimIO · Updated October 9, 2026 · 6 min read

The DS-160 asks for a digital photo, and its photo check is strict: a picture that looks fine to you can be refused because it is 610 × 400 pixels, 260KB, or your head is a little too small in the frame. This guide puts the official rules in one place, turns the percentages into pixels and explains the one rule almost nobody mentions: a photo can also be compressed too much.

Everything below comes from the U.S. Department of State's photo pages (checked October 2026). Your embassy or consulate may add its own instructions, for example a printed photo for the interview. If they differ, follow the official pages.

The rules at a glance

RuleRequirement
Shape and pixelsSquare. At least 600 × 600, at most 1200 × 1200 pixels
FileJPEG, 240 kB or less
ColourColour (24 bits per pixel), sRGB. Black-and-white is refused
CompressionCompression ratio of 20:1 or less
Head sizeTop of the hair to the bottom of the chin: 50–69% of the image height
Eye height56–69% of the image height, measured up from the bottom
RecentTaken within the last 6 months, showing how you look now
BackgroundPlain white or off-white
PoseFull face, facing the camera, neutral expression, both eyes open
GlassesNot allowed since November 1, 2016 (rare medical exceptions with a signed statement)
HatsNot allowed unless worn daily for religious reasons, with the full face visible
EditingNo digital changes to your appearance, not even removing red-eye in an editor

Photos copied or scanned from another official document, such as a driver's licence, are not accepted either.

The percentages in pixels

"50–69% of the image height" is hard to check by eye. On the two allowed extremes it works out like this:

ImageHead height (hair to chin)Eye line, from the top
600 × 600300–414 px186–264 px
1200 × 1200600–828 px372–528 px

The eye line is easier to read from the top: 56–69% up from the bottom is the same as 31–44% down from the top. On a 600-pixel photo your eyes should sit roughly a third of the way down, not in the middle. Photos where the eyes are in the middle of the square are the most common near-miss, because that is how most people centre a face.

The 20:1 rule: why a tiny file can fail too

Most advice stops at "under 240KB", but the digital image page also says the compression ratio should be 20:1 or less. The compression ratio is the size of the raw image divided by the file size. A raw 600 × 600 colour image is 600 × 600 × 3 bytes = 1,080,000 bytes, so:

ImageRaw sizeAllowed file size (20:1 to 240KB)
600 × 6001,080,000 bytes54–240KB
1200 × 12004,320,000 bytes216–240KB

Two practical lessons follow. First, 600 × 600 is the easy size: it has a wide window, while a 1200 × 1200 photo has to land in a 24KB gap. A bigger photo is not a better photo here. Second, "compress as much as possible" is the wrong instinct. Squeezing a 600 × 600 photo to 30KB goes past 20:1, and heavy compression is also what makes skin blotchy and edges blocky.

Smooth, retouched photos compress so easily that even a high-quality save can land under 54KB. When we tested SlimIO with a retouched-looking sample against a plain wall, a high-quality JPEG (quality 92) came out at 39KB — about 28:1. The US visa preset now raises the quality for photos like that until the file is at least 54KB; the same photo comes out at 58KB, about 18.6:1, still far under 240KB.

Scanning a printed photo: 2 × 2 inches at 300 ppi

If you have a printed US visa photo, the rules are 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm) scanned at 300 pixels per inch, which gives exactly 600 × 600 pixels. Be careful with photo studios that sell a "5 × 5 cm" print: 5 cm at 300 ppi is only 591 pixels, just under the 600 minimum. Scan at a higher resolution and crop to the square, or ask the studio for the digital file. The arithmetic behind this is in KB, pixels, cm and DPI explained.

Taking the photo yourself

The State Department recommends a professional photo service and warns that many phone and webcam photos are not good enough. If you take it yourself, these remove most problems:

  • Background: a plain white or off-white wall, nothing behind your head.
  • Light: even light from the front, such as facing a window. No shadow on your face or on the wall behind you.
  • Camera: someone else holds it at eye level, a few steps away, then you crop. An arm's-length selfie makes the nose look bigger and is often tilted.
  • You: no glasses, hair off the eyes, neutral expression, both eyes open, everyday clothes (no uniform).
  • No filters: phone beauty modes and portrait blur count as altering the photo. Turn them off.

If it still won't upload

You can finish the DS-160 anyway. The State Department explains that if your confirmation page shows an X instead of your photo, the upload failed, and you should bring one printed photo that meets the requirements, with the confirmation page, to the embassy or consulate where you apply. Check that post's instructions first. If the confirmation page shows your photo, the upload worked and no separate photo is needed. Some posts ask for a printed photo at the interview even after a successful upload, so read your appointment instructions.

Other forms want different numbers: Korean, Indian and many other applications use 3.5 × 4.5 cm or exam-specific sizes. For those, use the ID photo tool, which takes pixel sizes and KB ranges pasted straight from the form.

Questions

What size is a US visa photo for the DS-160?

A square JPEG of at least 600 × 600 and at most 1200 × 1200 pixels, 240 kB or less, in colour. The head (top of the hair to the chin) must be 50–69% of the image height and the eyes 56–69% of the height up from the bottom.

Is 240KB 240,000 bytes or 245,760 bytes?

The State Department doesn't say. Staying under 240,000 bytes is safe either way, and SlimIO's 240KB limit uses that number.

Can I wear glasses in a US visa photo?

No. Since November 1, 2016 glasses are not allowed in new visa photos, except in rare medical cases with a signed statement from a medical professional.

What if my DS-160 photo upload fails?

If the confirmation page shows an X instead of your photo, the upload failed. The State Department says to bring one printed photo that meets the requirements, with the confirmation page, to the embassy or consulate, and to check that post's instructions.

Can I take the photo with my phone?

You may take it yourself, but the State Department recommends a professional photo service and warns that many phone and webcam photos are not good enough. If you do it yourself, use even daylight, a plain white wall and someone else holding the camera at eye level.
