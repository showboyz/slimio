UPSC applications now run on upsconline.nic.in, and its upload page checks more than kilobytes: the file name, colour, how much of the photo your face fills, and later a live camera shot against your upload. Many websites still give the previous portal's rules. Here is what the portal checks today, what its error messages mean, and how to make both files on a phone.

Based on UPSC's portal instructions for the photo and signature (February 2026), its upload page, the 2026 exam notices and PIB press releases, checked November 2026. The 2027 notices are not out yet; if they say something different, follow them.

The upload page counts 1KB as 1,024 bytes, so the photo may be 20,480 to 204,800 bytes; aim for the middle of each range. Keep a natural expression, without a grin or raised eyebrows. Clear glasses are not banned, but avoid glare on them. In the forces or the NCC? Use a photo in ordinary clothes, because photos in uniform are not accepted.

The 75% face rule, in pictures

UPSC's sample sheet marks two ordinary passport photos, suit and shoulders included, as rejected because the face covers less than three quarters of the picture. It also rejects a turned head, a blurred or upside-down photo, a small face in a big frame, and a face so close that the ear lobes are cut off. The accepted examples are tight head shots: hairline at the top, chin near the bottom, the sides of the face close to the edges.

The upload page also runs a face check on the server. A loose crop gets the message "The face is unclear or doesn't meet the required 3/4th or 75% face coverage."

Make the photo

  1. Stand against a plain white wall, facing a window, and have someone take the picture at eye level. No cap, hair off your eyes, both ears showing.
  2. Open the UPSC photo preset and add the picture. A 450 × 600 frame appears with an oval guide.
  3. Use the zoom slider and drag until your head fills the frame: hair at the top, chin near the bottom.
  4. Check the size under the result and press ↓ Download.

Our test photo came out at 450 × 600 pixels and 34.4KB, saved as photo.jpg. The tool runs in your browser; the photo is not uploaded. It only crops and compresses, so it won't whiten a grey wall.

Make the three-signature image

  1. Take a sheet of plain white paper with no lines and a black pen.
  2. Sign three times, one below the other, the same way each time, leaving clear space so the signatures don't touch. The upload box also offers a template image to download, with the lines numbered 1, 2 and 3.
  3. Photograph the sheet from straight above in daylight, upright, with all three signatures in the picture and no shadow of your phone.
  4. Open the UPSC signature preset and add the photo. It whitens the paper, crops around the signatures and saves a 500 × 500 JPG within 20–100KB named signature.jpg.

Our sample sheet gave 500 × 500 pixels, 47.3KB, made in the browser like the photo. More on photographing a signature: signature images for online forms.

Fix the file name on a phone or PC

If photo.jpg is already in Downloads, a second download usually becomes photo (1).jpg, which the portal refuses. Delete the old file first, or rename the new one: open your phone's Files app, go to Downloads, press and hold the file, pick Rename from the menu, and change the name to photo, keeping .jpg at the end. On Windows, file extensions are often hidden; type only photo, because typing photo.jpg there creates photo.jpg.jpg, which also fails.

Live photo and the face check at the centre

The live photo button in the Common Application Form stays disabled until the photo is uploaded. Use the computer's webcam, or scan the QR code and take it on your phone. UPSC says the live photo "shall be matched" with the uploaded one; on a mismatch you cannot continue.

The current notices also make face authentication at the venue compulsory. According to PIB, invigilators use a phone app that compares your face with the photo from your application, in about 6–8 seconds. So upload a recent photo that looks like you, and arrive early.
