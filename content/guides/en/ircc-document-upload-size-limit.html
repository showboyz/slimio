Canada's immigration accounts take one file per upload field, and each file has a ceiling: 4 MB in most accounts, 5 MB in the IRCC Portal, and 2 MB in a few places, including IRCC's technical web form. Six pages of a bank statement photographed with a phone came to 16 MB in our test, so the upload is refused. This page lists the official limits for each account, shows how many scanned pages we could fit into 4 MB while keeping them readable, and gives the order of steps that gets a stack of scans under the limit.

The limits come from IRCC's Help Centre and its official web form (checked 8 November 2026). IRCC can change them, and the upload screen has the final say: if it shows a different number, go by the screen.

Not sure which account you are in? Aim for under 4 MB: that passes every row except the 2 MB ones. Two details hide in IRCC's lists: none of them includes HEIC, the format iPhones save photos in by default, and the secure account lists .doc but not .docx. The PR card photo in the PR confirmation portal has its own rule: JPEG or PNG, 715 × 1000 to 2000 × 2800 pixels, no larger than 4 MB.

Why an upload goes wrong even under the limit

  • Only the last file in a field survives. IRCC's words: "If you try to upload multiple files in the same field, only 1 (the last one you upload) will be saved." Upload page 1 and then page 2 to the same field and IRCC keeps page 2 alone. Put every page in one PDF first. The IEC instructions repeat this for police certificates: one field, all certificates in a single file.
  • The limit applies to the combined file. Four documents of 2 MB each make an 8 MB PDF once merged. Compress after merging, not before.
  • "MB" isn't defined. IRCC doesn't say whether 4 MB is 4,000,000 or 4,194,304 bytes. Windows counts in units of 1,024 and a Mac in units of 1,000, so a 4,100,000-byte file reads "3.91 MB" on one and "4.1 MB" on the other. Stay under 4,000,000 bytes and the question disappears.
  • The fallback route is smaller. If the portal won't take a document and you report it through IRCC's technical web form, that form allows 2 MB per file.
  • Colour may be required. A grey scan is smaller, but some instructions ask for colour; the IEC work permit page says to "include readable colour scans of all the supporting documents". Read your own checklist before switching to black and white.
  • Uploaded is not sent. When IRCC asks for a document in the secure account, it shows "Uploaded - not submitted to IRCC" until you sign and click Transmit.

How much fits in 4 MB: our measurement

We made a 24-page test bank statement (a made-up bank, labelled as a test) as phone-style photos: 3024 × 4032 pixels, about 2.7 MB per page, one photo per PDF page, the way many scanner apps save them. On 8 November 2026 we ran different page counts through SlimIO's 4MB and 2MB pages:

1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes here. Every file kept all its pages. *Over 50MB, so it was compressed in the browser rather than on the server.

So a 4 MB field holds about a dozen phone-photo pages at 210 dpi, sharper than the 96 or 150 DPI IRCC suggests, and 18 pages at about 178 dpi. Beyond that, quality drops fast: 24 pages fit only at 108 dpi. With a 2 MB limit, 6 pages kept 210 dpi and 12 pages fell to about 120.

One more number: the same six statement pages saved as a PDF from a web page, like a statement downloaded from online banking, came to 72.5 KB with no compression at all. If your bank offers a PDF download, use it instead of photographing paper.

Getting under the limit, step by step

  1. Photos of paper (JPG, PNG or iPhone HEIC): add them to JPG to PDF with a 4MB limit. It builds one PDF and shrinks the photos until the whole file fits. Our 12 photos (32.2 MB) became a 3.7 MB PDF in about two seconds, at 2000 pixels on the long side. It runs in your browser; the photos are not uploaded.
  2. Several PDFs for one field: join them with Merge PDF, which also runs in your browser, then compress the merged file.
  3. One PDF over the limit: use Compress PDF to 4MB, or to 2MB for a 2 MB field. It aims for just under 4,000,000 bytes by shrinking the images inside, so typed text stays text; only if that can't reach the target are pages turned into images, and the result tells you. Files up to 50MB go to SlimIO's server over HTTPS for this step and are deleted right after; bigger files are compressed in your browser. IRCC says it can't guarantee the security of third-party tools and asks you to read their privacy policy, so here is ours.
  4. Still too big: take out blank pages with Delete Pages, but keep every page your checklist asks for. IRCC's help pages say to combine documents into one file and don't describe splitting one document across fields, so check the instructions next to the field before you split anything.
  5. A PDF that asks for a password when opened, as some bank e-statements do: the compressor can't open it. If you know the password, remove it first with Unlock PDF, which works in your browser.

Mac users: Preview's Reduce File Size filter can make a text PDF bigger, as our iPhone and Mac test found. Compare the sizes before you replace anything. What compression changes inside a PDF is explained in compress a PDF without losing quality.

Before you press upload

  • Read the smallest print. Open the new PDF, zoom to 100% and check account numbers and dates.
  • Count the pages. Every page should still be there, in order.
  • Keep the file name plain. For the interest to sponsor form, IRCC allows only English or French letters, numbers, spaces, hyphens and a few symbols; a name like bank-statement-2026.pdf avoids the problem.
  • Finish the step. In the secure account, sign and transmit after uploading a requested document.
  • If it still fails, IRCC asks you to report the problem through its technical web form, with screenshots of the page and the error. Keep each attachment under 2 MB.
