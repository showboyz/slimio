Near the end of the Indian e-Visa form you upload two files: a recent photo of your face as a JPEG, and the bio page of your passport as a PDF. Both have a size window, and the photo also has to be a perfect square, so the passport-style photo most people already have is usually refused on the first try. This guide collects the rules from the official e-Visa site and its photo upload page, explains the three error messages you can get, and shows how to make both files from ordinary phone photos.

Everything below comes from the Government of India's e-Visa site, indianvisaonline.gov.in (checked October 2026). Apply only on that site. Rules and fees can change, so check the official pages again just before you apply.

The upload page adds two warnings: do not crop the passport image to use it as your recent photograph, and the application is liable to be rejected if the uploaded photograph is not clear and as per the specification.

You may see "1000 × 1000 pixels" and "300KB" quoted for the photo elsewhere. Those numbers come from the photo guide for the regular (paper) visa, not the e-Visa. The e-Visa photo page gives only the 350 × 350 minimum and the 10KB–1MB range.

Image size must be greater than 10KBThe file is under 10,000 bytesCompress less, or start again from a larger original

Mind the 1MB edge. The upload page counts 1MB as 1,000,000 bytes and 10KB as 10,000 bytes. Windows Explorer shows sizes in units of 1,024 bytes, so a file it lists as "990 KB" is 1,013,760 bytes and fails. iPhones save photos as HEIC by default, which is why the first error is so common. For how pixels, KB and print sizes relate, see KB, pixels, cm and DPI explained.

Make the photo from a phone picture

We tested this with a portrait phone photo (3024 × 4032 pixels, 653.7KB, a fictional person) on SlimIO's ID photo tool:

  1. Open Compress ID Photo and add your photo.
  2. Under Resize, choose Custom size in pixels… and enter Width 600, Height 600.
  3. Set Target size to Under 300KB and Format to JPG — works everywhere.
  4. Drag the square frame and use Zoom so your whole head, hair to chin, sits in the middle with a little space above and below.

The result was a 600 × 600 JPEG of 34.2KB (35,042 bytes), comfortably inside 10KB–1MB. SlimIO counts a size limit in decimal (1KB = 1,000 bytes), so even its "Under 1MB" setting stays under the e-Visa limit. The photo is processed in your browser and never uploaded. The tool crops and resizes; it does not change the background or retouch your face, so take the photo against a white wall in good light.

The US visa 600×600 px (square) preset also gives you a square, but its eye band follows US rules; for India, the custom size is the cleaner choice. The US rules themselves are in US visa photo requirements.

Turn the passport page into a PDF under 300KB

No scanner? Photograph the bio page with your phone and convert it to PDF. The catch is size: phone photos are often over 1MB, and the limit is 300KB.

We made an obviously fake "sample passport page", photographed at phone resolution (3024 × 2268 pixels, 1.24MB), and put it through JPG to PDF with a 300KB limit: choose Under 300KB as the PDF size limit, press Convert to PDF, then Download PDF. The PDF came out at 243.3KB (249,170 bytes). To fit, the tool re-saved the photo at 2000 pixels on the long side, and zooming into the PDF the name, passport number and dates were still sharp.

  • All four corners: shoot straight down so the whole page is in the frame.
  • No glare: the laminated page reflects light; turn the flash off and light it from the side.
  • Same passport: if you renewed your passport, use the new one in both the form and the upload.

The e-Tourist fee table (dated 8 September 2026) lists one fee per country, and the 30-day visa has separate columns for April–June and July–March, so the price can depend on when you travel. After you apply, check your inbox, including spam, the next day.

Separately from the visa, the official Indian Visa Su-Swagatam app is used to submit the e-Arrival card within 72 hours before arrival. That is arrival information, not a visa.
