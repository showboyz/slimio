The online OCI application on ociservices.gov.in asks for a photo, a signature image and your documents as PDFs, and the photo and signature come back when you update your OCI after a new passport. HCI London and CGI Auckland both name poor photos and signatures among the most common reasons for rejection, and official pages don't even agree on the numbers. Here are the current ones, where the older ones come from, and how to make files that pass.

Sources: the Ministry of Home Affairs pages on ociservices.gov.in and Indian embassy and consulate pages, checked in November 2026. The upload screens sit behind the portal login; if your screen shows a different number, follow the screen.

200KB or 500KB? Why the numbers disagree

Search for the OCI photo size and you will find three sets of numbers, all from official pages of different years:

  • 30KB, 380 × 380 to 900 × 900 pixels: an earlier version of the portal, still visible in screenshots inside some embassy PDFs such as the Santiago checklist.
  • 500KB, 200 × 200 to 1500 × 1500 pixels: older mission checklists such as Santiago's. CGI Auckland still prints both numbers, and the Embassy in Stockholm still says 500KB.
  • 200KB, 200 × 200 to 900 × 900 pixels: the Ministry of Home Affairs FAQ. Its PDF edition dated 22 July 2021 already had these figures, and the High Commission in Mauritius repeats them in its 2026 guide to the new login portal.

The Ministry runs the portal, and a file that meets its rule also meets the looser 500KB checklists. So: JPG, under 200KB, at most 900 pixels on a side. A 1200 × 1200 phone crop passes the old checklist and fails the current rule.

The photo, rule by rule

Official wordingWhat to do
Square, height and width equalCrop to a square. A 35 × 45 mm passport-style photo is the wrong shape
Not less than 51 × 51 mm, "80% coverage of face"Frame it tight: head and top of the shoulders. The Ministry's photo sheet gives the head, hair to chin, 25–35 mm of the 51 mm height: up to about two-thirds
Plain light colour background (not white), no borderStand in front of a light grey, cream or pale blue wall. Trim any white frame a studio print has
Front view, full face in the middleLook straight at the camera, head level, not tilted
No distracting shadows on the face or backgroundFace a window and step away from the wall
GlassesClear glasses are fine without glare or frames over the eyes; no tinted lenses

"Not white" catches people who reuse a passport photo shot on a pure white backdrop; at a studio, ask for light grey or cream. Head coverings are allowed only for religious reasons, with the whole face showing. No retouching or smoothing.

Children: babies under one don't need open eyes, and for children under ten the face size and eye position rules are relaxed. The child must still be alone in the picture: no hands, toys or chair backs.

The signature

The Ministry's FAQ only says the signature image is a JPG of up to 200KB. The shape comes from embassy and consulate instructions: the image should be three times as wide as it is tall, at least 200 × 67 pixels, and at most 900 × 300 (Buenos Aires) or 1500 × 500 (Santiago, Auckland). Pages call this ratio 1:3 or 3:1 (the Santiago checklist uses both in one box), but the pixel numbers settle it: wide, not tall. 600 × 200 fits every version.

  • Sign in black or blue ink on plain white paper. CGI Auckland asks for it to match the signature in your passport.
  • Make it large and dark, cropped close. HCI London's bad samples are very small, smudged and faint, or small and pushed to one side.

A child who can't sign yet

Missions agree on the main rule: a minor who cannot sign gives a left thumb impression in blue or black ink, and parents must not sign in the child's box. HCI London and the Embassy in Stockholm put the line at below 5 years; children who can write their name sign themselves, and parents sign their own part of the form. Press the left thumb on an ink pad and then on white paper, photograph it, and make it like a signature: a wide 600 × 200 JPG with the print in the middle.

Document PDFs

The Ministry's FAQ lists the documents but gives no file size. HCI London (2025) and the Embassy in Stockholm say under 1MB per PDF; Buenos Aires and Mauritius (2026) say 500KB. Keep each PDF under 500KB and you are covered either way; if the upload screen shows its own limit, that one wins.

  • Phone photos of pages: make one PDF per document with JPG to PDF under 500KB, in your browser.
  • Several PDFs for one category: join them with Merge PDF, also in your browser. HCI London suggests scanning at 100–200 dpi.
  • A scan that is too big: Compress PDF to 500KB. The file (up to 50MB) goes to SlimIO's server over HTTPS for a text-preserving step and is deleted right after; the image step runs in your browser.

Check each file before uploading: a CGI Toronto walkthrough of the new portal shows the note "Document Re-Upload limit is 5 after which Re-Upload is not allowed."

New passport? The same uploads again

The Ministry's Miscellaneous FAQ says an OCI cardholder must update the OCI each time a new passport is issued, whatever their age, uploading the new passport and a latest photo. It is free within 3 months of getting the passport; after that a US$25 fee applies, though the Ministry has waived it until 31 December 2026. CGI Houston adds that the passport update is online only and that physical OCI cards stopped being printed from 8 July 2026; you download an e-OCI card instead.
